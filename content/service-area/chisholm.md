Chisholm is another Iron Range mining town with a housing stock that reflects its history. Most of the homes here date to the early and mid-1900s, built during the same era as neighboring Virginia and Eveleth. The construction patterns are similar - compact floor plans, full basements, and materials that were standard for the time.

## What We Look for in Chisholm

The age of the housing stock puts the same items on the checklist as the other Range towns: electrical systems that may have been partially updated, plumbing materials approaching or past their expected lifespan, and foundations that have been through decades of Iron Range freeze-thaw cycles.

Heating is always a priority up here. The winters are long and cold, and the heating system is the hardest-working piece of equipment in the house. We evaluate the age and condition of the furnace, check the venting, and let you know if the system is still in its prime or in its bonus years.

Small-town homes on the Range often have additions, enclosed porches, or converted garages that were done by previous owners over the years. The quality of that work varies. We pay attention to how additions connect to the original structure - the framing, the roofline transitions, and whether the foundation work was done to the same standard as the original house.
