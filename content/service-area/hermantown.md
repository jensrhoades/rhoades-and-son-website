Hermantown is where Duluth spreads west. The housing stock here is generally newer than what you find in Duluth's hillside neighborhoods, with more mid-century ranch homes and split-levels from the 1960s through the 1980s, plus newer development on the edges.

## What a Home Inspection Looks for in Hermantown

Mid-century homes have their own set of things to watch for. Electrical panels from the 1960s and 1970s, including certain manufacturers that are now known to be problematic. Original windows that have long passed their useful life. Forced-air heating systems that may be on their second or third furnace, with ductwork that's never been updated.

Newer construction in Hermantown is generally straightforward to inspect, but "new" doesn't mean "no issues." Builder-grade materials have shorter lifespans than most homeowners expect. Grading and drainage around newer homes sometimes settles in ways that direct water toward the foundation rather than away from it. Having framed, insulated, and roofed houses ourselves, we know what the finished product is supposed to look like from the inside out. When something was done fast or done wrong, we can usually tell.

## Same Weather, Different Houses

Hermantown gets the same Lake Superior-influenced weather as Duluth - the moisture, the wind, the snow loads. The difference is that the housing stock is better matched to the climate because most of it was built after modern insulation and ventilation standards. That said, we still check attic ventilation, ice dam potential, and frost footing depth on additions and garages. The 60-inch footing requirement applies everywhere in this part of the state.
