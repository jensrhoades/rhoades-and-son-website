Hibbing has a construction history unlike anywhere else in the service area. The town was physically relocated in the early 1900s when the Hull-Rust-Mahoning Mine expanded, and many of the homes in what's now central Hibbing date to that relocation era, the 1920s through the 1940s. The median construction year across town is 1955, and about 28% of the housing stock predates 1940.

That relocation-era housing was built during a period of rapid construction. Some of it was done carefully. Some of it was done fast. The quality varies, and 100 years later, the differences show up clearly during an inspection.

## What a Home Inspection Covers in Hibbing

The older neighborhoods have the standard pre-war checklist: potential knob-and-tube wiring, galvanized plumbing, and foundations that have been through a century of freeze-thaw. But Hibbing also has a significant stock of 1950s and 1960s homes that bring their own issues - electrical panels from manufacturers now considered unreliable, early forced-air systems, and insulation that doesn't meet current standards.

Heating systems are a priority in every Hibbing inspection. The Iron Range gets some of the coldest sustained temperatures in Minnesota. Furnaces here don't get a break from October through April. We evaluate the age, condition, and operation of the heating system, the condition of the heat exchanger where visible, and the venting. If a system is in its bonus years - still working but past its expected lifespan - we let you know so you can plan for it.

## Hibbing's Housing Market

Median home values in Hibbing are around $130,000, with recent sales around $165,000. The market moves slower here than in Duluth or the metro, which gives buyers more time but also means homes may sit longer between owners. A house that's been on the market for a while, or one that's been a rental, gets extra attention during the inspection.

About 71% of Hibbing homes are owner-occupied, one of the higher rates in the service area. Owner-occupied homes tend to be better maintained, but that's a generalization, not a guarantee. The inspection tells you what's actually there.
