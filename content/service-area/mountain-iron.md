Mountain Iron sits right next to Virginia and shares the same Iron Range housing characteristics - older construction, long winters, and heating systems that earn every dollar of their operating cost. The town's population is around 3,000, and the housing stock is a mix of mining-era homes and some newer development.

## What We Look for in Mountain Iron

The same patterns that apply across the Iron Range apply here. Pre-war and mid-century homes with original or partially updated electrical, aging plumbing, and foundations that have been working against frost and water for decades. We evaluate each system based on what we can see and document it with photos and specific locations.

Properties near the mines can have unique ground conditions. Mining activity changes the subsurface over time, and while most residential areas are well outside active zones, it's part of the context we carry when inspecting Range homes. The 60-inch frost footing requirement applies to all construction here, and additions and garages are the most common places where footing depth may not meet current standards.
