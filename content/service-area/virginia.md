Virginia has about 4,900 housing units, and the median construction year is 1952. Over a third of the homes here were built before 1940 - mining-era construction from when the Iron Range was booming. These homes were built with the materials and methods of their time, and many of them were built well. But they've been through 80-plus years of the coldest winters in the state, and what's behind the walls has been replaced, patched, or left alone depending on who owned the house along the way.

## What a Home Inspection Looks for on the Iron Range

Iron Range homes have a character all their own. The older housing stock typically features full basements with poured or block foundations, original hardwood framing, and floor plans designed around a central heating source. What we're evaluating is how well each system has aged and what's been done to update it over the decades.

Electrical is a big one. Homes from the 1930s and 1940s may still have original wiring, or they may have been rewired at some point. Partial rewiring, where the main panel was updated but the branch circuits were left alone, is common and creates its own problems. We check the panel, trace what we can see, and note the condition.

Heating systems work harder in Virginia than almost anywhere else in the state. Furnaces here run from October into April, sometimes May. That kind of use means shorter equipment lifespans. We evaluate the age, condition, and operation of the heating system and let you know where it sits in its lifecycle. If a furnace is working fine but it's 22 years old, you should know that before you close.

## Virginia's Housing Market

The median sale price in Virginia is around $121,000, well below the state average. Lower home values mean lower entry points for buyers, but they also mean that previous owners may have been working with limited budgets for maintenance and updates. An inspection tells you which corners were cut and which systems were kept up, so you can make an informed offer.

About 56% of Virginia homes are owner-occupied, with the rest rentals. If you're purchasing an investment property or a rental, the inspection is about understanding what it will cost to keep the property safe and functional for tenants.
