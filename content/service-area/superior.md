Superior shares a harbor with Duluth and shares the same old housing stock. This is a port and railroad city with homes dating back to the late 1800s and early 1900s. The construction patterns are similar to what you find across the bridge - pre-war framing, older foundations, and decades of Lake Superior weather working on every surface.

## What a Home Inspection Covers in Superior

The housing here is predominantly older single-family homes. The issues we see most often are the same ones that show up in any pre-war housing stock: original wiring that may or may not have been updated, galvanized plumbing with decades of interior corrosion, and foundations that have been through a hundred freeze-thaw cycles.

Superior's home values tend to run lower than Duluth's, which means two things for buyers. First, you're more likely to find something in your budget. Second, lower-value homes sometimes come with deferred maintenance, because previous owners were working with the same budget constraints. An inspection tells you what's been kept up and what hasn't, so you can factor repair costs into your offer rather than discovering them after closing.

## Lake Superior Weather Applies on Both Sides

The lake doesn't care about the state line. Wind, moisture, snow loads, and ice damming are the same issues in Superior as they are in Duluth. We check attic ventilation, insulation coverage, and roof condition the same way on both sides of the harbor. Deep frost footing requirements apply in Douglas County just as they do across the bridge.
