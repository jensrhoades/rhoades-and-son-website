What I'm looking at during an inspection

These are real photos from real inspections. This is the kind of thing that gets missed when your inspector hasn't built houses.

Hail damage to shingles

The bruising shows up as dark circles where the granules got knocked off. Once those granules are gone, the mat underneath breaks down fast. This roof is on borrowed time.

Dangling wire, potentially hot

An exposed wire with no cap, no box, no protection. This is a primary safety hazard. Not something to put on a list for later.

Porch foundation crumbling

Freeze-thaw cycles did this. Water gets into the concrete, freezes, expands, and breaks it apart over years. In Minnesota, this is something I'm always looking for.

Active water damage in attic

This is current, not old. You can tell by the color and the dampness. Could be a roof leak, could be condensation from poor ventilation. Either way, it needs to be traced and fixed before it rots the sheathing.

Improper GFCI wiring

A GFCI outlet that's wired wrong won't trip when it needs to. Near water, that's a serious problem. I test every one I can reach.

Cracked window pane

Sometimes obvious, sometimes hidden by curtains or blinds. A cracked pane means the seal is gone too, which means you're losing heat all winter.

Drain waste leak

Slow leaks under sinks cause more damage than fast ones because nobody notices them. By the time the floor feels soft, the subfloor is already compromised.

Lifted shingle with moss growth

Moss holds moisture against the shingle surface. Over time it works under the edges and lifts them. Once a shingle lifts, water gets underneath and the damage accelerates.

T&P relief valve not routed to floor

If this valve pops, the discharge needs to go to the floor or a drain. Not into the air, not sideways, not capped off. This is a safety item, not a cosmetic one.

What's not included

An inspector can only report on what he can physically see. I can't see behind walls, under floors, or underground. If I can't access an area, it won't be in the report, and I'll tell you that clearly.

I don't currently offer septic system checks or radon testing. Both are on the plan for the future. For now, I refer those to qualified specialists. That is their wheelhouse, not mine.

I also don't provide cost estimates for repairs or advice on whether to buy or not buy. I identify problems and give you the information. What you do with it is your decision.
