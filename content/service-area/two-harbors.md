Two Harbors has some of the oldest housing stock on the North Shore. About 42% of the homes here were built before 1940, and the median construction year is 1951. These are ore dock and railroad-era homes, built when the town was a shipping hub. The construction was solid for the time, but these houses have been standing through North Shore weather for the better part of a century.

## What a Home Inspection Looks for in Two Harbors

The age of the housing stock here means we're frequently looking at original foundations, older electrical systems, and plumbing that may have been patched or partially updated over the decades. Partial updates are sometimes worse than no updates at all, because the old and new systems don't always play well together.

Two Harbors also has a vacancy rate around 13%, higher than most of the region. A lot of that is seasonal and second-home properties. Homes that sit empty through Minnesota winters deal with specific problems: frozen pipes if the heat drops, critter entry through gaps that open up as materials expand and contract, and settling that goes unnoticed because nobody is there to catch it early. If you're buying a property that's been used seasonally, that context changes what we pay attention to.

## North Shore Weather and Your Home

Full Lake Superior exposure means wind, moisture, and snow loads that are more severe here than even in Duluth. Ice damming is common, and it's almost always a ventilation issue in the attic, not a roofing material issue. We check soffit and ridge vents, look at insulation coverage, and note where heat is escaping.

The 60-inch frost footing requirement applies here. Decks, additions, and garages are the common spots where footings may not have been done to code, especially on older properties or owner-built additions.
