Cloquet's housing stock has a story behind it. The 1918 fire burned most of the original town, and the rapid rebuild that followed shaped what's here today. The median construction year is 1964, but about 27% of the homes predate 1940 - meaning a significant chunk of the housing stock is actually 1920s-era reconstruction. Homes built during a rapid rebuild period can vary in quality. Some were constructed carefully. Others went up fast to get people housed.

The town has about 5,600 housing units and a population around 12,500. It's a paper mill town, with Sappi as the major employer, and the housing reflects a working community that's been here for generations.

## What a Home Inspection Covers in Cloquet

The mix of construction eras here means no two inspections look the same. A 1925 rebuild-era home has different priorities than a 1960s ranch or a newer home on the edges of town.

For the older stock, we're checking the same things we look at across the region: electrical that may have been partially updated, plumbing materials and their condition, and foundation integrity after a century of use. The 1920s rebuild homes in particular can have surprises, because speed of construction sometimes meant shortcuts that aren't visible until you look closely.

Mid-century homes in Cloquet, the 1950s through 1970s, make up a large portion of the housing. These are generally solid construction, but they're now 50 to 70 years old, and major systems are at or past their expected lifespans.

## Cloquet's Climate

Cloquet is about 20 miles from Lake Superior - far enough inland to lose some of the direct lake effect, but close enough that the moisture influence is still real. Winters are long and cold, and the 60-inch frost footing requirement applies. We check additions, garages, and decks for adequate footing depth, especially on older properties where construction predated current standards.
