Grand Rapids is the outlier in the region. While the Iron Range mining towns have housing stocks from the 1930s through the 1950s, Grand Rapids has a median construction year of 1978. Only about 8% of homes here predate 1940. This is a regional service center with steady growth over the decades, and the housing reflects that - more variety in construction eras and styles than the mining towns.

## What a Home Inspection Covers in Grand Rapids

Newer housing stock means different priorities. Instead of knob-and-tube wiring and galvanized plumbing, we're more often looking at homes from the 1970s through the 1990s. This era introduced some materials and methods that have since proven problematic - certain electrical panel brands, early versions of engineered products, and insulation practices that didn't account for moisture management the way current building science does.

Homes from the 2000s and later make up a decent share of Grand Rapids inventory. Newer construction is generally straightforward to inspect, but it's not immune to problems. Builder-grade materials have predictable lifespans, and grading around newer homes sometimes settles in ways that direct water toward the foundation.

## Grand Rapids Is Different from the Range

The housing conversation here is genuinely different from Virginia or Hibbing. Where those towns are mostly about evaluating 80-year-old systems, Grand Rapids is about mid-life homes where the first round of major replacements is coming up or already due. Roofing, siding, water heaters, furnaces - these all have lifespans, and a 1985 home is now 40 years old. We let you know where each system sits in its lifecycle so you can budget accordingly.
