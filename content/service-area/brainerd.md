Brainerd's housing market has a pattern that's different from anywhere else in our service area. The median construction year is 1971, which makes the typical home here significantly newer than what you find in Duluth or on the Iron Range. But the real story isn't the age of the housing stock - it's the cabin-to-permanent-home conversion that's been happening here for decades.

Lake properties that were built as summer cabins in the 1960s and 1970s are now being sold as year-round residences. A structure designed for three months of use is a fundamentally different building than one designed for twelve. The insulation, the heating system, the plumbing, and the foundation were all sized and specified for seasonal use. When that property gets listed as a permanent home, the inspection has to address whether the structure can actually handle a full Minnesota winter.

## What a Home Inspection Covers in the Brainerd Area

About 21% of Brainerd's housing predates 1940, so there is older stock in the original town center. These homes share the same characteristics as older construction across the region - systems that have been through multiple rounds of updates, with varying quality of work.

But the majority of homes here are mid-century and later. The 1970s and 1980s construction that makes up a large portion of the market is now 40 to 50 years old. That's the age where the first generation of major systems - roofing, siding, furnaces, water heaters - has been replaced at least once, and the second round may be coming due. We evaluate where each system sits in its lifecycle so you can plan ahead.

Mobile homes make up about 4.7% of Brainerd's housing stock, higher than most communities in the service area. Manufactured homes have their own inspection considerations, from the frame and tie-down system to the specific mechanical standards they're built to.

## Sandy Soils and Water Tables

The Brainerd Lakes area sits on sandy, glacial soils with water tables that can be high near the lakes. Sandy soil drains fast, which is generally good for foundations, but high water tables near lakeshores can create moisture intrusion issues in lower-level spaces. We check for water evidence in basements and crawl spaces and note where the grade directs water around the foundation.
